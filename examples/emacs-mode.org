#+TITLE: Emacs mode: keys and what they do
#+STARTUP: showall

Each example shows a buffer before (=‸= marks the caret), the keys typed, and the buffer after. Open this file in the app with Emacs mode on, put the caret where =‸= is, type the keys and compare. The results are what Emacs 29.3 (Org 9.6.15) gives, and a test in this project checks that the app gives the same. The full list of keys is in the README's Emacs mode section.

* Moving
Keys that only move the caret. A count goes before the key.

** M-f goes to the end of the next word
Keys: ~M-f~

#+begin_example
one ‸two three
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
one two‸ three
#+end_example

** C-n keeps the column across a short line
Keys: ~C-n C-n~

#+begin_example
abcdef
abcde‸f
ab
abcdefgh
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
abcdef
abcdef
ab
abcde‸fgh
#+end_example

** M-e goes to the end of the sentence
Keys: ~M-e~

#+begin_example
One thing.  ‸Then another.  Last.
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
One thing.  Then another.‸  Last.
#+end_example

** C-u 3 M-f repeats the move
Keys: ~C-u 3 M-f~

#+begin_example
‸one two three four
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
one two three‸ four
#+end_example

* Killing and yanking
What a kill takes goes on the kill ring (and the device clipboard); =C-y= puts it back.

** C-k kills to the end of the line; a second C-k kills the line break
Keys: ~C-k C-k~

#+begin_example
abc‸ def
ghi
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
abc‸ghi
#+end_example

** M-d kills the next word
Keys: ~M-d~

#+begin_example
one ‸two three
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
one ‸ three
#+end_example

** M-k kills to the end of the sentence
Keys: ~M-k~

#+begin_example
One.  ‸Two words.  Three.
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
One.  ‸  Three.
#+end_example

** M-z kills through the next character you type
Keys: ~M-z x~

#+begin_example
one ‸two xray
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
one ‸ray
#+end_example

** C-<backspace> kills the word before the caret
Keys: ~C-<backspace>~

#+begin_example
one two‸ three
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
one ‸ three
#+end_example

** C-y puts back what a kill took
Keys: ~C-k C-y~

#+begin_example
one ‸two
three
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
one two‸
three
#+end_example

* The mark and the region
=C-SPC= sets the mark; the region is the text between the mark and the caret.

** C-SPC, a move and C-w kill the region
Keys: ~C-SPC M-f C-w~

#+begin_example
one ‸two three
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
one ‸ three
#+end_example

** C-x h marks the whole buffer
Keys: ~C-x h C-w~

#+begin_example
one
‸two
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
‸
#+end_example

** M-@ marks the next word without moving the caret
Keys: ~M-@ C-w~

#+begin_example
one ‸two three
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
one ‸ three
#+end_example

* Case, spacing and lines
Commands that tidy text without selecting it.

** M-u upcases the word
Keys: ~M-u~

#+begin_example
one ‸two three
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
one TWO‸ three
#+end_example

** M-c capitalizes the word
Keys: ~M-c~

#+begin_example
one ‸two three
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
one Two‸ three
#+end_example

** M-t swaps the words around the caret
Keys: ~M-t~

#+begin_example
one two‸ three
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
one three two‸
#+end_example

** C-t swaps the characters around the caret
Keys: ~C-t~

#+begin_example
ab‸cd
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
acb‸d
#+end_example

** C-x C-t swaps the line with the one above
Keys: ~C-x C-t~

#+begin_example
first
second‸
third
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
second
first
‸third
#+end_example

** M-^ joins the line to the one before
Keys: ~M-^~

#+begin_example
one
  ‸two
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
one‸ two
#+end_example

** M-\ deletes the spaces around the caret
Keys: ~M-\~

#+begin_example
one   ‸  two
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
one‸two
#+end_example

** C-x C-o leaves one blank line
Keys: ~C-x C-o~

#+begin_example
one


‸
two
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
one
‸
two
#+end_example

** C-o opens a line and leaves the caret
Keys: ~C-o~

#+begin_example
one ‸two
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
one ‸
two
#+end_example

** C-M-o splits the line and keeps the caret on the column
Keys: ~C-M-o~

#+begin_example
one ‸two
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
one ‸
    two
#+end_example

** C-x C-u upcases the region after Emacs asks "Use this command?"
Keys: ~C-SPC M-f C-x C-u y~

#+begin_example
‸hello world
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
HELLO‸ world
#+end_example

* Filling
=M-q= fills to 70 columns, as =fill-column= is in Emacs.

** M-q fills the paragraph to 70 columns
Keys: ~M-q~

#+begin_example
The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog while the sun sets slowly behind the hills, and the evening air turns cool‸ over the quiet fields.
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog while the sun sets slowly
behind the hills, and the evening air turns cool‸ over the quiet
fields.
#+end_example

** M-q keeps the indent and the bullet of a list item
Keys: ~M-q~

#+begin_example
- The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog while the sun sets slowly behind the hills,‸ and the evening air turns cool over the quiet fields.
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
- The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog while the sun sets
  slowly behind the hills,‸ and the evening air turns cool over the
  quiet fields.
#+end_example

* Org: RET, lists, tables and headings
=RET= is Org's =org-return=, so it depends on where the caret is.

** RET at the end of a list item starts the next line under its text
Keys: ~RET~

#+begin_example
- item‸
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
- item
  ‸
#+end_example

** RET in the middle of a heading splits it
Keys: ~RET~

#+begin_example
,* Heading ‸title
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
,* Heading
‸title
#+end_example

** RET in a table goes to the next row
Keys: ~RET~

#+begin_example
| ‸a | b |
| c | d |
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
| a | b |
| ‸c | d |
#+end_example

** M-<right> demotes a list item
Keys: ~M-<right>~

#+begin_example
- one
- t‸wo
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
- one
  - t‸wo
#+end_example

** C-c C-c aligns a table
Keys: ~C-c C-c~

#+begin_example
| a | bbb |
| cccc | d‸ |

after
#+end_example

becomes

#+begin_example
| a    | bbb |
| cccc | ‸d   |

after
#+end_example
