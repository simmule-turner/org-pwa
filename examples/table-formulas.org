#+TITLE: Table formulas
#+STARTUP: showall
#+CONSTANTS: tax=0.08 g=9.81

A cookbook for =#+TBLFM:= formulas. Each table holds its formula on the line below it. Use *🔢 Calc* under a table (or the command palette's *Recalculate all tables*; in Emacs, =C-c C-c= on the formula line or =C-u C-c *=). The results stored here are what Emacs 29.3 (Org 9.6.15) computes, and the app gives the same; the last section says where the two differ.

* Row and column totals
Column formulas (=$N=) skip the rows above the first rule. A cell formula (=@N$M=) writes one cell.

| Item   | Price | Qty | Total |
|--------+-------+-----+-------|
| Pens   |  1.50 |   4 |  6.00 |
| Books  | 12.00 |   2 | 24.00 |
| Paper  |  4.25 |   3 | 12.75 |
|--------+-------+-----+-------|
| Totals |       |   9 | 42.75 |
#+TBLFM: $4=$2*$3;%.2f::@5$3=vsum(@2..@4)::@5$4=vsum(@2..@4);%.2f

* Named columns and parameters
A row marked =!= names the columns, a row marked =$= holds parameters, and =#= marks the rows to calculate. =tax= below also comes from the =#+CONSTANTS:= line at the top; the table's own parameter wins.

| ! | Item              | Price | Qty |   Tot |
|---+-------------------+-------+-----+-------|
| $ | tax=0.1           |       |     |       |
| # | Pen               |  1.50 |   4 |  6.60 |
| # | Book              | 12.00 |   2 | 26.40 |
| / | note: not counted |       |     |       |
#+TBLFM: $5=$Price*$Qty*(1+$tax);%.2f

* Ranges between rules
=@I..@II= is the data between the first two rules. =@>= is the last row and =$>= the last column.

| Region | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | Year |
|--------+----+----+----+----+------|
| North  | 12 | 15 | 11 | 18 |   56 |
| South  |  9 | 14 | 10 | 13 |   46 |
| West   | 20 | 18 | 22 | 25 |   85 |
|--------+----+----+----+----+------|
| All    | 41 | 47 | 43 | 56 |  187 |
#+TBLFM: $6=vsum($2..$5)::@>$2..@>$6=vsum(@I..@II)

* Statistics
| Test | Score |
|------+-------|
| A    |    72 |
| B    |    88 |
| C    |    95 |
| D    |    61 |
| E    |       |
|------+-------|
| Sum  |   316 |
| Mean | 79.00 |
| Min  |    61 |
| Max  |    95 |
| Mid  |    80 |
| N    |     4 |
| SD   | 15.38 |
#+TBLFM: @7$2=vsum(@2..@6)::@8$2=vmean(@2..@6);%.2f::@9$2=vmin(@2..@6)::@10$2=vmax(@2..@6)::@11$2=vmedian(@2..@6)::@12$2=vcount(@2..@6)::@13$2=vsdev(@2..@6);%.2f

The blank score in row E is left out of every aggregate, so the mean is over four scores.

* Number formats
|      Value |     Fixed | Scientific | Engineering |
|------------+-----------+------------+-------------|
| 123456.789 | 123456.79 |     1.23e5 |       123e3 |
| 3.14159265 |      3.14 |     3.14e0 |      3.14e0 |
#+TBLFM: $2=$1;f2::$3=$1;s3::$4=$1;e3

* Rounding and math functions
|    x | floor | ceil | round |  sqrt |     x^2 |    ln |
|------+-------+------+-------+-------+---------+-------|
|  2.5 |     2 |    3 |     3 | 1.581 |    6.25 | 0.916 |
| 7.25 |     7 |    8 |     7 | 2.693 | 52.5625 | 1.981 |
|   16 |    16 |   16 |    16 | 4.000 |     256 | 2.773 |
#+TBLFM: $2=floor($1)::$3=ceil($1)::$4=round($1)::$5=sqrt($1);%.3f::$6=$1^2::$7=ln($1);%.3f

* Conditions and grades
Comparisons give 1 or 0. Nested =if= is an else-if chain; here it gives grade points.

| Student | Exam | Lab | Points |
|---------+------+-----+--------|
| Ana     |   92 |  88 |      4 |
| Ben     |   81 |  60 |      3 |
| Cy      |   55 |  90 |      0 |
| Dee     |   74 |  71 |      2 |
#+TBLFM: $4=if($2 >= 90 && $3 >= 80, 4, if($2 >= 80, 3, if($2 >= 60 && $3 >= 60, 2, 0)))

* Dates
Subtracting two timestamps gives the days between them.

| Start            | End              | Days |
|------------------+------------------+------|
| [2026-01-05 Mon] | [2026-03-20 Fri] |   74 |
| [2026-02-10 Tue] | [2026-02-24 Tue] |   14 |
#+TBLFM: $3=$2-$1

* Durations
Flags =T=, =U= and =t= read =H:MM= cells as durations and show =H:MM:SS=, =H:MM= or decimal hours.

| Task 1 | Task 2 | Total (T) | Total (U) | Hours (t) |
|--------+--------+-----------+-----------+-----------|
|   2:12 |   1:47 |  03:59:00 |     03:59 |      3.98 |
|   0:45 |   3:30 |  04:15:00 |     04:15 |      4.25 |
#+TBLFM: $3=$1+$2;T::$4=$1+$2;U::$5=$1+$2;t

* Constants
=g= comes from the =#+CONSTANTS:= line at the top of this file.

| Mass kg | Weight N |
|---------+----------|
|       2 |    19.62 |
|      10 |    98.10 |
#+TBLFM: $2=$1*$g;%.2f

* Several formulas, in order
Formulas do not run in the order written. Org sorts them by their target, and runs column formulas row by row before field formulas, so a formula sees the results of the ones that sort before it. Here =$3= is written first but runs after =$2=.

| Base | Doubled | Plus ten |
|------+---------+----------|
|    3 |       6 |       16 |
|    8 |      16 |       26 |
#+TBLFM: $3=$2+10::$2=$1*2

* Named fields
A row marked =_= names the field below each of its cells; a row marked =^= names the field above. A name can then stand for that field on the right of a formula, or be the target on the left: =$tax== writes that one field.

|   | Price | Tax   | Total  |
| _ | price | tax   | total  |
| # | 100   | 20.00 | 120.00 |
#+TBLFM: $tax=$price*0.2;%.2f::$total=$price*1.2;%.2f

* A default format
A =$= row can hold =%== and a format. It is added to a formula that has flags such as =N= but no format of its own, so one line sets the decimals for the table. A formula with no flags at all does not get it.

| $ | %=%.2f |   |      |
| # |      1 | 3 | 0.33 |
| # |      2 | 7 | 0.29 |
#+TBLFM: $4=$2/$3;N

* Complex numbers
| z1     | z2     | Product  |
|--------+--------+----------|
| (1, 2) | (3, 4) | (-5, 10) |
#+TBLFM: $3=$1*$2

* Solving equations
Letters are variables. =solve(equation, variable)= solves for one of them, taking the positive root where there are two; an inequality keeps its direction, and turns around when the divisor is negative. A root of a negative number is complex.

| Equation    | Solved for x    |
|-------------+-----------------|
| a*x+b=c     | x = (c - b) / a |
| x^2-5*x+6=0 | x = 3           |
| sqrt(x)=3   | x = 9           |
| 2^x=8       | x = 3.          |
| -2*x>6      | -3 > x          |
| x^2=-4      | x = (0, 2)      |
#+TBLFM: $2=solve($1,x)

=fsolve= writes the choice between roots as =s1=. A list of equations and a list of unknowns give a system.

| Equation | Both roots     |
|----------+----------------|
| x^2=a    | x = s1 sqrt(a) |
| x^2=4    | x = 2 s1       |
#+TBLFM: $2=fsolve($1,x)

| Equations      | Unknowns | Solution                               |
|----------------+----------+----------------------------------------|
| [x+y=3, x-y=1] | [x, y]   | [x = 2, y = 1]                         |
| [x+y=a, x-y=b] | [x, y]   | [x = a + (b - a) / 2, y = (a - b) / 2] |
#+TBLFM: $3=solve($1,$2)

* Simplify and expand
=simplify= applies Calc's rules for sums, products, powers, roots, logarithms and trig functions; =expand= multiplies out sums.

| Expression  | simplify        | expand        |
|-------------+-----------------+---------------|
| x+y+x       | y + 2 x         | x + y + x     |
| (x+1)^2     | (x + 1)^2       | x^2 + 2 x + 1 |
| (x+1)*(x-1) | (x + 1) (x - 1) | x^2 - 1       |
#+TBLFM: $2=simplify($1)::$3=expand($1)

* Derivatives
=deriv(expr, x)= differentiates with Calc's rules, and a third argument puts a value in for x. A function Calc has no rule for comes back as =f'(x)=.

| Expression  | deriv(x)           |
|-------------+--------------------|
| x^3+2*x     | 3 x^2 + 2          |
| x*exp(x)    | exp(x) + x exp(x)  |
| ln(x)/x     | x^-2 - ln(x) / x^2 |
| sqrt(x^2+1) | x / sqrt(x^2 + 1)  |
#+TBLFM: $2=deriv($1,x)

* Intervals and chained comparisons
An interval has a bracket or parenthesis at each end for closed or open. It adds, multiplies and takes powers as a whole, and =in(x, interval)= tests membership. =1 <= x < 7= is the test =x in [1 .. 7)=.

| Interval | Times 2   | Squared   | Contains 3? |
|----------+-----------+-----------+-------------|
| [1 .. 5] | [2 .. 10] | [1 .. 25] |           1 |
| (0 .. 4] | (0 .. 8]  | (0 .. 16] |           1 |
#+TBLFM: $2=$1*2::$3=$1^2::$4=in(3,$1)

| x | 1 <= x < 7 |
|---+------------|
| 3 |          1 |
| 7 |          0 |
#+TBLFM: $2=1 <= $1 < 7

* Bars in cells
=orgtbl-ascii-draw= draws a bar for a value between a minimum and a maximum.

| Item | Sales | Bar          |
|------+-------+--------------|
| Mon  |    12 | WWWWV        |
| Tue  |    30 | WWWWWWWWWWWW |
| Wed  |    24 | WWWWWWWWWl   |
#+TBLFM: $3='(orgtbl-ascii-draw $2 0 30 12)

* Text from if
Calc writes a text result as character codes, so ="P"= shows as =[80]=. Use numbers when the result should read well (the grades table above does).

| Score | Result |
|-------+--------|
|    95 | [80]   |
|    40 | [70]   |
#+TBLFM: $2=if($1>=50,"P","F")

* Number formats
=f=, =n=, =s= and =e= format a float with that many decimals or digits, in fixed, normal, scientific and engineering style. A result that is a whole number is left alone. A float shows 8 digits by default, a whole float keeps its dot, and very small or large floats switch to scientific. =%.Ne= and =%g= are C-style.

|      Value |        f2 |      n3 |      s3 |     e3 |     %.2e |   Default |
|------------+-----------+---------+---------+--------+----------+-----------|
| 123456.789 | 123456.79 | 123000. |  1.23e5 |  123e3 | 1.23e+05 | 123456.79 |
|   0.000314 |    3.1e-4 | 3.14e-4 | 3.14e-4 | 314e-6 | 3.14e-04 |   3.14e-4 |
|        2.5 |      2.50 |     2.5 |   2.5e0 |  2.5e0 | 2.50e+00 |       2.5 |
#+TBLFM: $2=$1;f2::$3=$1;n3::$4=$1;s3::$5=$1;e3::$6=$1;%.2e::$7=$1*1

* Division, remainder and fractions
=/= and =%= bind looser than =*=. =%= takes the sign of the divisor, =\= divides and rounds down, and a division by zero is left as written. =F= keeps fractions exact, written with a colon.

|  A | B |  A/B |   A%B |   A\B | A/B (F) |
|----+---+------+-------+-------+---------|
| 14 | 4 |  3.5 |     2 |     3 |     7:2 |
| -7 | 2 | -3.5 |     1 |    -4 |    -7:2 |
|  5 | 0 |  5/0 | 5 % 0 | 5 \ 0 |     5/0 |
#+TBLFM: $3=$1/$2::$4=$1%$2::$5=$1\$2::$6=$1/$2;F

* Dates and day numbers
A day number is counted from 1 January of year 1. =date= turns a date into that number and a number back into a date; adding to the bare date keeps it a date.

| Date             | Plus 30          | Day number | Back to date     |
|------------------+------------------+------------+------------------|
| [2026-01-15 Thu] | [2026-02-14 Sat] |     739631 | [2026-01-15 Thu] |
| [2026-03-31 Tue] | [2026-04-30 Thu] |     739706 | [2026-03-31 Tue] |
#+TBLFM: $2=$1+30::$3=date($1)::$4=date($3)

* Where Emacs and the app differ
Everything above gives the same result in both. These give a different result in Emacs 29.3:
- Digits above Calc's own accuracy: =sqrt(2);p50n50= ends in =...875377= in Emacs and =...8753769= in the app, which is the correct root.

App only: =uconv($1, "mi/hr", "m/s")= converts units (Emacs uses Calc's =u c= command); =#+PLOT:= draws inline (Emacs needs gnuplot and writes an image file); =date-to-time=, =format-time-string= and =now()= follow the device clock and the browser's date parser.
